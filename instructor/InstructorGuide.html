Instructor Guide

Low-Rank Structure Is Geometry - Paul Breiding and Se Eun Choi
Scope boundary. This curriculum does not present tensor decomposition as an alignment algorithm. It studies the mathematical conditions under which low-rank representations may become useful evidence in interpretability and alignment auditing.

The teaching arc

Interpretation
Auditability

Do not let the class jump directly from a small reconstruction error to a semantic claim. The sequence deliberately makes learners identify the represented object, its coordinate symmetries, the model's structural assumptions, and the numerical evidence before discussing neural concepts.

Alignment evidence ladder

1 Reconstruction
The selected model approximates the selected data object.
2 Invariance
Coordinate symmetries and identifiability limits are accounted for.
3 Conditioning
Small perturbations do not create uncontrolled changes.
4 Stability
The finding persists across seeds, perturbations, ranks, and solvers.
5 Behavior
External examples, labels, or held-out tasks support the claim.
6 Intervention
Changing the feature produces the predicted effect under controls.
Key sentence: Good reconstruction alone stops at Step 1.

Before the session

Reusable facilitation prompts

Predict. What should change and what should stay invariant?
Observe. Which visual or diagnostic tests that prediction?
Explain. Is the result about the object, coordinates, model, or optimizer?
Limit. Which evidence-ladder step has actually been reached?

Lab-specific emphasis

Lab 1 - invariance before interpretation

Have learners name the unchanged matrix before discussing the changing factors. Transfer the same reasoning to reciprocal CP scaling and component permutation.

Lab 2 - assumption before labeling

Require a sentence beginning “This component is interpretable only if ...”. Separate structural capacity from whether a finite solver run reaches that structure.

Lab 3 - stability before trust

Connect the small Gram eigenvalue to difficulty assigning contribution between nearly identical components. Stress that the plateau flag records slow progress only; distance and conditioning are separate evidence used to diagnose its cause.

Lab 4 - a factor is a hypothesis, not a label

Have learners connect one candidate direction to several high-usage crops, then perturb its coefficient. Nonnegativity supports an additive reading but proves neither uniqueness nor semantics. Clearly label the sample × location × feature model as a TensorKitchen extension inspired by CRAFT.

Assessment and debrief

Ask what the evidence warrants

Use the exercise sheet for observations; use the ladder for claims

Final group report

Ask each group to choose one notebook result and report five items:

  1. the represented object;
  2. the relevant coordinate equivalence;
  3. the model assumption;
  4. the conditioning or stability evidence;
  5. the behavioral or intervention test still required.

Do not award full credit for “the reconstruction error is low” unless the question asks only about Step 1.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionInstructor response
The displayed factor is unique.Ask which rescalings, permutations, or basis changes preserve reconstruction.
Nonnegativity proves semantic meaning.Treat it as a structural assumption and request external validation.
The best finite-run error identifies the best model.Separate model capacity, optimization success, and scientific meaning.
A plateau means convergence.Compare recent improvement with gradient, conditioning, and component activity.
A stable feature is causal.Stability is Step 4; intervention is Step 6.
